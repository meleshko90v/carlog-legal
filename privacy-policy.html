Records stored on your device

CarLog stores information you enter, including vehicle make/model/year, VIN, odometer, maintenance descriptions, expenses, currencies, dates, reminder details, service/place links and preferences. These records are kept in a SQLite database in the app's private Android storage. Notification plans also use private app storage. CarLog does not read mileage from Bluetooth, OBD equipment or GPS.

CarLog has no account service, advertising SDK, analytics backend or cloud-sync endpoint. Its release app does not request Android's INTERNET permission. Android services and other apps you choose can use their own network connections as described below.

Free version 1.0

All existing CarLog features are available free of charge in version 1.0. There are no advertisements, subscriptions, in-app purchases or payment integrations. CarLog does not process payment details. Any future change to data handling will require an updated privacy policy and applicable Google Play disclosures before that change is released.

Notifications

Android alarms and notification channels display reminders. Reminder titles and vehicle information may appear on the lock screen according to your Android settings. You can deny notifications, disable channels or revoke optional exact-alarm access. Android permissions, power management and device availability can affect delivery time.

Optional approximate location

CarLog asks for optional approximate location permission to show your current city. Denying permission does not prevent record keeping or reminder use. Location is requested while an app screen is visible, on launch/resume or when you request refresh. There is no continuous tracking or background location service.

Android location and Geocoder services process the approximate fix to obtain a city. Depending on the device/provider, these system services may use a network backend under the provider's own privacy policy. Coordinates are transient within this lookup; CarLog does not write them to records, logs or backups, and does not send them to a CarLog server. A recent device fix may be reused for up to ten minutes. The city label is held in memory for no more than five minutes, and expires sooner when its fix is too old. Failed/denied lookups clear the label. A private flag avoids repeating the automatic permission request. You can retry or manage permission through Settings.

Current device location is separate from the service/place you select for a record and the timezone used for reminders.

Places and Google Maps

Selecting a service/place in Google Maps is optional and user initiated. CarLog opens the Maps app or browser with your place text/search query or saved Maps link. Google processes that interaction under its own terms and privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy . This feature does not require CarLog's location permission.

If you share a place back to CarLog, only the text/link you share and confirm is stored with the record. A private draft preserves unfinished form inputs while Maps is open. It is cleared when the form closes, the place is consumed or a new request replaces it. Requests older than 24 hours are discarded at the next Maps interaction. An unassigned shared place stays locally until used or replaced.

Export and import

New user-facing exports are password-protected .carlog files containing your records and settings. The file uses ChaCha20-Poly1305 authenticated encryption with a 256-bit key, a fresh 32-byte random salt and 12-byte nonce, and PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations to derive the key from your password. Encryption also authenticates the format header. The logical JSON file-size/record validation limits remain unchanged; encrypted transport adds an 80-byte header/tag overhead. Encryption protects the exported file, not the app's SQLite database or an unlocked device.

Choose a strong password of 12–256 characters and keep it securely. CarLog does not persist, transmit or log backup passwords, and has no password-recovery service. Passwords and key material are used transiently in process memory; complete erasure of every runtime/keyboard memory copy cannot be guaranteed. CarLog cannot recover a forgotten password. Anyone who obtains both the backup and password can read the backup.

Legacy unencrypted JSON v1/v2 backups remain importable after a warning. Existing JSON copies remain unencrypted; importing them does not encrypt or delete those external files. The current user interface does not offer unencrypted export.

Android's system file picker lets you choose a destination or source. A Files/cloud document provider you select may upload, retain or share that file under its own policy. A provider receives the encrypted file for a new export, not its password; legacy files selected for import can contain plaintext personal data. CarLog cannot remove copies held by those providers or other people.

New encrypted exports stream directly into encrypted private temporary files without creating a plaintext JSON export file. Import validates bounded records in a disposable private SQLite staging database. Encrypted data must reach authenticated end-of-file before the transaction replacing primary records can begin. A wrong password, corrupted file, cancelled prompt or failed validation does not authorise replacement of current records. Primary replacement remains transactional.

Imported records and staging rows are not separately encrypted at rest by CarLog: they use Android's private app storage protection, like the primary SQLite database. Temporary files are removed where possible; interruption or cleanup failure can leave private staging/input files until Android clears cache or app storage is cleared. CarLog does not promise secure erasure from flash media. The app opts out of Android automatic backup/device transfer for private records and reminder state. Manual password-protected export is the supported backup method.

Retention and deletion

Records remain on your device until you delete them. You can delete individual entries, or delete a vehicle and its related records after confirmation. Android Settings > Apps > CarLog > Storage > Clear storage removes the app's private records and settings. Uninstalling removes private app storage under Android's normal behaviour.

Exported backups are independent copies. Delete them separately in the Files/cloud-provider app, including its trash/history where applicable. Importing a backup does not delete external copies. CarLog has no online account or account-deletion endpoint.

Keep your device and backup files protected. Contact the publisher above with questions about CarLog's handling of your information.
